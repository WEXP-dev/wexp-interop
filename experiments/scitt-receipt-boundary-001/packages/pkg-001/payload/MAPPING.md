# Mapping: a SCITT Receipt as the boundary finding of a Core-02 appraisal

One page. The appraisal is `draft-sergeev-wexp-core-02` (semantics `wexp-core-2`, XML SHA-256
`de983051a1c6ea51ea9b7f965fe9eabf2d97af92ab2f074781bd44e3f99c8782`); the transparency
architecture is RFC 9943. This mapping is local to this case: profile identity
`wexp-work/interop/scitt-receipt-boundary-001/mapping-1`, not a SCITT profile and not a
registered WEXP mapping profile.

## What the Receipt establishes

RFC 9943, section 6.3: "A TS MUST ensure that a Signed Statement is registered before releasing
its Receipt." Section 7: "When a Signed Statement is registered by a TS a Receipt becomes
available." Registration is defined as the TS's steps 1-5 of section 6.3, which include "The TS
MUST perform signature verification [...] of the Signed Statement" and "The TS MUST check the
attributes required by a Registration Policy are present in the protected headers."

So a verified Receipt establishes exactly one predicate: **the Transparency Service identified by
the receipt's signing identity registered these Signed Statement bytes under its registration
policy at the recorded time** (section 7: "The Registration time is recorded as the timestamp when
the TS added the Signed Statement to its VDS"). For the pinned emulator the predicate is weaker
still, for three reasons carried together under one limitation atom
`L-ts-no-issuer-signature-check`: (1) the emulator's registration does not verify the issuer
signature and applies no registration policy (its own source comment, SOURCE-PIN.md); (2) its
"inclusion proof" is computed over a fresh tree built per receipt from 63 constant dummy leaves plus
the real leaf, so it proves inclusion in a synthetic one-shot tree, not in a persistent append-only
log, and the registration itself is a file write; (3) the accepted identity is a self-signed service
certificate generated by the same emulator process in the same run and copied out of its workspace,
so the relying party and the Transparency Service are the same operator and the trust anchor is
accepted by fiat. Under RFC 9943 section 9.2 a registration "only proves it was produced by an
Issuer"; this emulator's receipt does not even establish that.

Core-02, section 10: "a transparency receipt supports only the exact predicate established by its
verification under the applicable profile; a promise of inclusion is not proof of inclusion, and
neither by itself establishes continuing custody, PROV, IV, or execution".

## Where it goes: `boundary_finding`

Core-02, section 7: "The Boundary Ceiling is an exclusion rule, not an evidence source. A boundary
finding can prevent a base from being supported; it MUST NOT create a positive base finding.
Observation itself requires a positive exact observation finding."

## Why the ceiling is `observation`

What was registered is a statement about the target, witnessed by a third party at a time. That
bounds the evidence to the fact that the statement was made and recorded. Core-02, section 10,
"a signature authenticates bytes under a key; it does not establish content truth or boundary
control", and RFC 9943, section 9.2: accuracy of statements is the issuer's responsibility, not
established by the TS. For this source the Receipt does not reach `intent`: the TS "MAY validate the Signed Statement payload" (section 6.3) and the
emulator does not, so nothing about the statement's content, let alone an intent to act, is
established by registration. Hence `ceiling_base = observation`: no base above observation can be
supported on this boundary (section 7: "supported_content_base <= accepted_boundary_ceiling").
Whether a receipt from a conformant Transparency Service that verifies the issuer signature and
applies a policy could warrant an `intent` ceiling is a question for a mapping profile; this case
does not settle it. Section 10's "observation does not imply intent, invocation, or execution" is a
non-inference rule about claims, cited below for what the Receipt does not establish, not as the
ground for the ceiling choice.

## What the Receipt does not establish

- Not `execution`, `invocation` or `intent` (Core-02 section 10, quoted above; section 7: the ceiling excludes, it does not add).
- Not an observation finding either: "Observation itself requires a positive exact observation finding" (section 7). Input (b2) shows the Receipt alone yields no supported claim.
- Not PROV or IV: section 10, "neither by itself establishes continuing custody, PROV, IV, or execution"; "IV does not imply PROV".
- Not coverage: section 10, "presence of records does not establish completeness of coverage" and, in the next bullet, "absence of a record does not establish nonoccurrence". Coverage is the separate `draft-sergeev-wexp-coverage-00`.
- Not content truth or boundary control: section 10, signature clause; section 7: "Attribution is not proof of actual boundary control."
